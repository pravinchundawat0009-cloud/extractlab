# Mango-leaf NADES trial — step by step

**Just follow the numbers. Nothing here is a choice.**

You will: make one batch of solvent → label four containers → put leaf in three, nothing in the
fourth → leave all four at 45 °C for 3 hours → centrifuge → hand four tubes to the HPLC lab.

That is the whole experiment. Everything below is just those five things, broken into steps you can
tick off.

**Total bench time: 2 half-days.** Part 1 takes ~3 hours (mostly waiting). Part 3 takes ~3.5 hours
(almost all waiting). You are not busy for most of it.

## Step 0 — Read this table before you weigh anything

These 8 numbers are the entire experiment. Everything else is procedure.

| What | How much | Into what |
|---|---:|---|
| Citric acid monohydrate | **143.47 g** | the big beaker |
| β-alanine | **60.83 g** | the big beaker |
| Purified water | **35.70 g** | the big beaker, in 5 portions |
| Dried mango-leaf powder | **0.500 g × 3** | containers M1, M2, M3 |
| Finished solvent | **30 mL in each of 4** | M1, M2, M3, BLANK |

| Nothing at all | — | container BLANK |

## Part 1 — Make the solvent (about 3 hours)

### Step 1 — Put on PPE and clear the bench

Goggles, nitrile gloves, lab coat. Citric acid dust stings eyes and fine powder irritates lungs.

**Do this now:** get out one 400–600 mL beaker, a glass rod or magnetic stirrer, a thermometer, a
watch glass or foil cover, and your balance.

You should see: an empty bench with only what you need. If the bench is crowded, clear it — you will
be reaching into a hot water bath later.

### Step 2 — Set the water bath to 70 °C and put a thermometer in the water

Set the dial to 70. Put a real thermometer in the water, not just the dial.

You should see: thermometer reads 70 ± 2 °C after 10–15 minutes.

If not: trust the thermometer, not the dial. Adjust until the thermometer reads 70. If it will not
reach 70, tell your supervisor before continuing — a bath at 60 will double your stirring time.
**Never go above 80 °C**; that starts breaking down the citric acid.

### Step 3 — Weigh 143.47 g of citric acid monohydrate into the beaker

Add it in 3–4 portions. Do not pack it down. Write down what you actually weighed.

You should see: roughly a third of the beaker filled with white powder.

If you cannot get exactly 143.47: write down what you got (e.g. 143.21) and keep going. Later you
can scale the other two ingredients — see "If your weights are not exact" at the end.

### Step 4 — Weigh 60.83 g of β-alanine into the same beaker

On top of the citric acid. Write it down.

You should see: white powder, no liquid yet.

### Step 5 — Stir the two powders together dry, for about 30 seconds

Use the glass rod. This stops them clumping when water goes in.

You should see: one uniform white powder, no visible lumps of a different texture.

### Step 6 — Add the 35.70 g of water in 5 small portions

**This is the step people rush and regret.** Add roughly 7 g, stir for 2 minutes, then the next 7 g,
and so on. Do not pour it all in at once.

You should see, in order: damp powder → thick slush → lumpy paste. **Lumps are normal.** They are
unmelted solid, not a mistake.

If you see: a puddle of water sitting separate from the powder — you added too fast. Stir hard and
wait before adding more.

### Step 7 — Put the beaker in the 70 °C bath, cover it loosely, and start stirring

Cover with a watch glass or a piece of foil. **Do not seal it with a screw cap or tight lid** — it
expands when hot.

Stir slowly. Fast stirring whips air in, and air bubbles look exactly like undissolved solid, which
makes it impossible to tell whether you are done.

**Write down the time you started.**

### Step 8 — Keep stirring at 70 °C until it turns clear. Expect 60–120 minutes.

This is the step where most people give up too early and decide the recipe failed.

Watch for this sequence:

| After | What you see |
|---|---|
| 15–30 min | Thick, lumpy, opaque paste. Still gritty if you rub a drop between your fingers. |
| 45–75 min | It suddenly turns glossy and starts to flow. This happens fast, not gradually. |
| 90–120 min | Clear amber liquid, like thin honey. Holds a thread when you lift the rod out. |

**Your stop rule: 2.5 hours maximum.** Then stop, whatever it looks like.

### Step 9 — Decide: is it clear?

Take a drop on a clean watch glass and look through it against something white.

| What you see | What to do |
|---|---|
| Clear, see-through amber, no grit on the rod | **Good. Go to step 10.** |
| Gritty, or crystals visible | **Stop. Do not add leaves. Do not add water.** Put a lid on it, label it, and take it to your supervisor. This is a real result, not a failure. |
| Still lumpy after 2.5 hours | Same as above — stop and report it. |

**Do not add extra water to make it clear.** That quietly changes the water content, which is the one
variable your whole experiment is built around. A failed batch that you report is worth more than a
"rescued" one you cannot describe.

### Step 10 — Cool it, then measure the density (10 minutes)

Cool to room temperature, uncovered.

Then: weigh a dry, empty 10 mL or 25 mL measuring cylinder. Fill it exactly to the mark with your
solvent. Wipe the outside. Weigh it again.

```
density = (full weight − empty weight) ÷ the volume you filled to
```

Do it twice and use the average. **Write it down — you need it in step 13.**

You should see: a number somewhere between 1.05 and 1.40 g/mL. If it is wildly outside that, re-check
your weighing of the water.

## Part 2 — Prepare the leaf (about 20 minutes, can run during Part 1)

### Step 11 — Start the moisture test

While the solvent is still stirring, weigh about **1.0 g** of leaf powder into a small, pre-weighed
dish. Write down the exact mass and the dish's empty mass.

Put it in the oven at **105 °C for 2 hours**. Write the start time on the dish.

**Why:** the final answer is "how much mangiferin per gram of DRY leaf", and this test is how you know
what "dry" means. It costs you no attention — it just sits there.

Take it out after 2 hours, let it cool in a desiccator, and weigh it. That is your moisture number.

### Step 12 — Weigh 0.500 g of leaf into each of three labelled containers

Label them **M1**, **M2**, **M3**. Weigh each one separately and write down each actual mass.

You should see: three containers each with half a gram of green-brown powder.

**Take your time here.** This number is the one you divide your final answer by. Being off by 0.04 g
is an 8 % error in your result.

---

## Part 3 — Run the extraction (about 3.5 hours, almost all waiting)

### Step 13 — Put the solvent into all four containers, by weight

Label the fourth container **BLANK**. Put **no leaf** in it.

For the other three, you already have the leaf in them.

Now use your density from step 10 to convert 30 mL into grams:

```
grams per container = 30 × your density
```

At a density of 1.20 g/mL that is **36.0 g per container**. Weigh it into each container.

**Weigh it, do not pipette it.** This solvent is thick like honey — a pipetted "30 mL" can easily be
26 mL because of what sticks to the inside. Weighing takes half the time and is actually correct.

Write down the mass you put in each of the four.

### Step 14 — Stir each container briefly to wet the powder

Ten seconds each with a glass rod, its own rod if you have four.

You should see: the powder mixes in rather than sitting in a crust on top.

**Why this matters:** dry powder dropped into thick solvent forms a skin that water never gets
through. Thirty seconds now saves you a sample that extracted badly for no recorded reason.

### Step 15 — Cover all four with the same kind of lid, equally tight

Same lid type on all four. Not "roughly the same" — the same.

**Why:** over 3 hours, a loose lid lets water evaporate. Different lids on different containers means
your samples had different water contents, and you would later blame that on the leaf.

### Step 16 — Set the bath to 45 °C and wait until it is stable

Change the bath from 70 to 45. Wait until the thermometer actually reads 45 ± 1 °C before anything
goes in.

### Step 17 — Put all four containers in. Write the time. Leave them alone for 3 hours.

All four go in **together**.

Then: **no shaking. No stirring. No ultrasound. Do not take them out to look at them.**

That stillness is the entire point of this experiment.

**Check the bath temperature at 1 hour and at 3 hours** — just read the thermometer, do not lift the
beakers out. A cold container drags the bath down and it may not recover on its own.

You should see: the liquid darkening over the 3 hours. That is extraction happening.

---

## Part 4 — Separate and hand over (about 1 hour)

### Step 18 — Take all four out and let them cool to room temperature

Uncovered is fine once they are out. Do not open them while they are still hot.

**Why:** warm liquid plus room air equals condensation, and then you have water you cannot account
for in your volumes.

### Step 19 — Centrifuge each one at 20 000 × g for 30 minutes

If your lab does not have an ultracentrifuge, use whatever filtration procedure your lab normally
uses — but use the **same** procedure for all four containers.

Write down what you used.

You should see: a solid pellet or filter residue at the bottom, and clearer liquid above.

If it will not filter: swap to centrifugation rather than forcing it through. Thick solvent plus fine
powder clogs filters.

### Step 20 — Pour off the liquid into labelled tubes and record the volume

New tube for each. Label with the container name and the date: **M1**, **M2**, **M3**, **BLANK**.

Record the volume you actually recovered from each one — it will not be exactly 30 mL.

### Step 21 — Store at −20 °C and fill in the HPLC request

Put all four in the freezer. Write the date.

Then give the HPLC lab **all four tubes** plus these four pieces of information:

1. These are three leaf extracts and one blank. **The blank must be injected at the same dilution** —
   it is how we show the mangiferin came from the leaf and not from the solvent.
2. Please clean the sample up before injecting: dilute 1:4 with water, load onto a 1 g C18 SPE
   cartridge, wash with 4 mL water, and elute the phenolics with 3 mL methanol + 0.1 % formic acid.
   **Do not inject the raw solvent** — it will foul the column.
3. Detection is at **254 nm** on a C18 column; mangiferin is the peak you are measuring.
4. What is your detection limit? I need to know how much to dilute.

## What the result should look like

When the numbers come back, the yield is:

```
mg mangiferin per g dry leaf = (sample − blank) × 30 × dilution ÷ 1000 ÷ (0.500 × (1 − moisture))
```

An honest expectation: if your leaf is 2 % mangiferin by weight and you recover half of it, the lab
should report roughly **40–70 µg/mL** in the diluted solution. If they report **under 5 µg/mL**,
suspect the dilution or the cleanup before you suspect the leaf. If they report something off the top
of their calibration curve, the number is meaningless and needs a re-injection.

The number that matters most is not the yield — it is **how closely M1, M2 and M3 agree with each
other**. That tells you whether 3 hours of stillness was long enough. If the three disagree by more
than about a third, the next experiment is to try a longer time, not a different recipe.

## If something goes wrong

| Symptom | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Solvent still gritty after 2.5 h | It never formed properly | Stop, label, tell your supervisor. Do not add water. |
| Crystals in the solvent the next morning | 20 % water is too dry for this mixture | Stop, label, tell your supervisor. This is a finding, worth reporting. |
| Two separate layers in the solvent | The water separated out | Do not use it. Tell your supervisor. |
| Sample will not filter | Too thick | Centrifuge instead, same for all four. |
| Less liquid recovered than expected | Normal — some stays in the pellet | Record it. Do not top it up with fresh solvent. |
| The three samples disagree a lot | 3 hours may be too short | This is a result. Report it; the next run adds a longer time point. |

## Where these numbers come from

So you are never confused about why a number is what it is. Three lines, no chemistry needed:

1. **The recipe is 1 part β-alanine to 1 part citric acid by molecule, with water making up 20 % of
   the total weight.**
2. **Citric acid monohydrate is already 8.58 % water.** So of your 20 % water, some arrives inside the
   acid and the rest you add yourself. That is exactly why the water figure (35.70 g) is not simply
   20 % of 240 g (which would be 48 g).
3. **Solving those two conditions together for a 240 g batch gives** 60.83 g β-alanine + 143.47 g
   citric acid monohydrate + 35.70 g added water. Total water is then 48.00 g out of 240 g = 20.0 %.
